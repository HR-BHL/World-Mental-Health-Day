Looking out for each other

Great teams notice

We work closely, often in busy and demanding moments. That means we are often the first to notice when a colleague is not quite themselves, and a simple, kind conversation can make a real difference.

Things you might notice

  • A colleague who is quieter than usual, or no longer joining in
  • Someone always skipping breaks, or always the last to leave
  • Small mistakes from someone who is usually very precise
  • Seeming tired, short-tempered or not quite themselves

And in yourself

  • Finding it harder to switch off after a shift
  • Sleeping less well, or feeling tired on days off
  • Less enjoyment in the parts of the job you usually love
  • Running on coffee and skipping proper meals
1

Find a quiet moment

A word on a break or on the walk to the station, away from the floor.

2

Ask twice

"Fine, thanks" is automatic. A second, gentle "How are you, really?" shows you mean it.

3

Listen

You do not need answers. Being heard is often the most helpful thing.

4

Share the support

Point them to this page, their manager or the HR Team.

5

Check in later

A quick message a few days on means a great deal.

Easy ways to start the conversation

You've seemed a bit quieter lately. Is everything all right? I'm on my break in ten minutes. Fancy a coffee? Thank you for telling me. What would help right now?
